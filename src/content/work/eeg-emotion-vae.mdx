## Problem

<p class="lede">The brain signal is rich; the label is one word.</p>

An EEG recording is many channels sampled many times a second, full of artefacts, and shaped by whoever happens to be wearing the cap. The emotion it is supposed to reveal is a single, coarse label. Training a classifier straight from raw signal to label means learning everything at once from very little supervision.

The project asks a narrower question first: can a model learn a compact description of the signal, and does that description carry information about emotion?

## Constraints

- **Public data.** Two established datasets: DEAP, EEG recorded while participants watched music videos and rated their own reactions, and SEED, EEG recorded while participants watched film clips chosen to be positive, neutral or negative.
- **Two toolchains.** EEG work lives in MATLAB as much as in Python, so the code uses both.

A variational autoencoder has two halves. The encoder maps a recording to a distribution over a few latent factors; the decoder has to rebuild the signal from a sample of them. Because everything must pass through that narrow space, the factors are pushed to capture what is consistent in the signal rather than its noise. Emotion detection then works on those factors instead of on the raw channels.
