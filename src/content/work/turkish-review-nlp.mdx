  approach="Label 885,000 reviews from Hepsiburada, a Turkish online marketplace, as positive or negative and compare TF-IDF logistic regression with recurrent models built on BERTurk and DistilBERT embeddings."

## The question

When does a pretrained transformer earn its cost? On English text the answer is usually "almost always". On Turkish, where suffixes stack onto a stem and a vocabulary built for English splits words badly, it is worth measuring instead of assuming.

- **Data:** 947,030 reviews with 1–5 star ratings. Three-star reviews were dropped as neutral; 1–2 stars became negative and 4–5 positive, leaving 885,480 reviews, 93 % of them positive.
- **Classical baselines:** TF-IDF on words and word pairs (10,000 features) with logistic regression, and a support vector machine.
- **Transformers:** the token embeddings of BERT-base-turkish (BERTurk) and DistilBERT-base-turkish, kept frozen, feed a bidirectional LSTM or GRU over the first 23 tokens of each review. The transformers themselves are not fine-tuned.

## Results

<Results metrics={frontmatter.metrics}>
  On this task the cheap model won. TF-IDF with logistic regression beat both transformer-embedding models by about half a point of accuracy, while each recurrent model needed five training epochs of 4–9 minutes. Accuracy flatters everyone here because 93 % of reviews are positive. The real difference shows in the minority class: the DistilBERT model found only 56 % of the negative reviews. The test splits also differ (20 % for BERTurk, 30 % for the others), so treat gaps under a point as noise. The notebooks are on [GitHub](https://github.com/walidhemati/Hepsiburada-review-classification-NLP).
</Results>

## What I'd do next

- Fine-tune BERTurk end to end instead of only reusing its embeddings. That is the comparison the question really asks for.
- Report macro F1 and negative-class recall on one shared split, so the imbalance cannot hide behind accuracy.
