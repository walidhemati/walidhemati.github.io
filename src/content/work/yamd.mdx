## Overview

Molecular dynamics moves atoms forward in time by computing the forces between them and integrating Newton's equations of motion, step after step. The equations fit on a napkin. The bugs hide in the details: a sign in a force, an off-by-one in a neighbour loop, a time step that quietly drifts the energy.

yamd is my coursework for the summer 2024 course Molecular Dynamics in C++ at the University of Freiburg. It started from the course's Meson template, which brought the build setup, Eigen and GoogleTest.

It simulates atoms in two settings. The first is a Lennard-Jones system, integrated with velocity Verlet, held at temperature by a Berendsen thermostat and sped up with cell-based neighbour lists. The second is gold, described by the Gupta (Ducastelle) embedded-atom potential: Mackay icosahedral clusters are heated until they melt, and a gold nanowire is pulled apart at a fixed strain rate, split across processes with MPI domain decomposition.

## How it is built

- **Eigen** for vectors and arrays, so the physics reads as linear algebra rather than index arithmetic.
- **GoogleTest** for unit tests, so each piece of the integrator can be checked on its own.
- **Meson** as the build system, from the course template.

## Result

Heating each cluster in small steps gives a temperature curve with a flat stretch where the cluster melts. Fitting that stretch gives the melting point and the latent heat. The melting point rises with size, from 807 K at 923 atoms to 869 K at 3,871 atoms, still well below bulk gold's 1,337 K, as expected for small clusters where many atoms sit on the surface.

The source is on [GitHub](https://github.com/walidhemati/yamd).

Its GPU successor in Python is [pymd](/work/pymd/), which reproduces yamd's forces to 1e-14 and runs the same systems on a GPU.
