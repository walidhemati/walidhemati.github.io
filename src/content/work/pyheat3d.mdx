## Problem

The explicit heat equation and the Jacobi iteration share one update. If Python reaches the hardware limit anywhere, it should be here, and the interesting question is what it takes to go past the single-step limit honestly.

## Approach

- Ladder: NumPy, PyTorch, `torch.compile`, Triton, Warp, Numba and CUDA C, all checked against a NumPy oracle.
- 3D kernels march along z, keeping the planes in registers.
- Temporal blocking (k = 2, 3, 4, 8) in CUDA C, reported with its traffic, not as a victory over DRAM.
- Validation: spatial order 1.97–2.08, time order 1.00 against analytic modes.

## Results

<Results metrics={frontmatter.metrics}>
  The single-step kernels written in Python sit at the copy bandwidth (2D) or within 10 % of it (3D). Against established codes on the same GPU: pystencils matches in 2D and is slower in 3D; AN5D's generated temporal blocking is faster in 2D and slower in 3D. See the [paper](https://github.com/walidhemati/pyheat3d/blob/master/paper/paper.pdf).
